## Design Context

### Users
Two "Abbots" (event admins/hosts), using the `/abbot` dashboard on their own phones, standing in a dim, loud pub, mid-event, likely a couple of drinks in themselves. They are not casual browsers — they're operating live infrastructure for 16 friends who are waiting on them (open the next Station, resolve a dispute, grant a stuck team entry) while also socializing. Every screen here is a tool used under mild time pressure and poor lighting, not a leisurely admin panel.

### Brand Personality
Belgian abbey / monastic manuscript, played straight but with a wink — "sacred bureaucracy for a pub crawl." Three words: **ceremonial, tactile, wry**. Reference: illuminated manuscripts, wax-sealed abbey correspondence, Trappist beer labels — not corporate quiz-night software, not Kahoot, not a generic admin dashboard. Anti-reference: anything that reads as a Bootstrap/shadcn admin panel with a theme skin bolted on top.

### Aesthetic Direction
Parchment (`--color-vellum`), oxblood/burgundy, gold leaf, ink-black. Blackletter (`UnifrakturMaguntia`) for display headings, EB Garamond for body text. The signature element is the **wax seal** (`WaxSeal.tsx`) representing each Station's state (sealed/open/broken), with a seal-crack animation on unlock. Light mode only, mobile-first (390px baseline), light Motion usage reserved for the seal break and leaderboard reveal — the rest of the UI should feel print-like and static, not app-like and animated.

### Design Principles
1. **The Abbot's screen is a control panel, not a report.** The most time-pressured, most-tapped action per section should be unmissable at a glance, even one-handed, even tipsy.
2. **Ceremony in the chrome, clarity in the controls.** Blackletter and parchment carry the theme; the actual buttons, states and data must stay legible and unambiguous — theme never gets to cost clarity.
3. **Everything reads correctly in one dim room.** Real contrast, real tap targets, no reliance on subtle color alone to convey state.
4. **One glance answers "what needs me right now?"** Open Stations, pending appeals, and anything blocking play should visually outrank everything settled/closed/resolved.
5. **Undo the ceremony for danger.** Irreversible actions (Close, Delete, Reset) already have confirm-steps — the design must make that confirm step visually distinct from routine ceremonial framing, not just another parchment box.
